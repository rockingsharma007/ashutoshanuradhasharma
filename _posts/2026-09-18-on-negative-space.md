We talk about design as if it were addition — what to put on the page, which
elements to include, what to add to make it better. But the choices that
separate a considered layout from a cluttered one are almost always
subtractions. The space you leave empty is not the absence of design. It *is*
the design, doing its work where no one thinks to look.

## What emptiness is for

Give a line of text room and it reads as important. Crowd it and it reads as
filler, no matter what it says. The eye takes its instructions from the gaps
as much as from the marks — where to rest, what to group together, what to
read first and what to read at all. Whitespace is not leftover. It's the
punctuation of a visual sentence.

Confidence shows up the same way. A page unafraid of its own emptiness tells
the reader that every remaining thing was chosen on purpose. Fear fills every
corner.

## A small exercise

Take any layout you admire and cover the content — the words, the images, all
of it. Look only at what's left: the margins, the gutters, the pauses. You'll
often find the empty regions are more deliberately proportioned than the
filled ones. That's not an accident of the good ones. It's the definition.

> The composer Debussy is supposed to have said that music is the space
> between the notes. Design is the space between the things.
